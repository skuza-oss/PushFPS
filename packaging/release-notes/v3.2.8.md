## What's new in 3.2.8: PushFlow 2's first two experiments

PushFlow 2 is the plan for the next generation of PushFPS's own frame generation. This version has its first
two parts in it. Both are **experiments that start off**: nothing changes for you until you switch one on,
and with both off PushFlow makes exactly the frames 3.2.7 made (the tests compare them picture for picture).

You find them in *Professional → Frame generation*, under the PushFlow tier, when PushFlow is the method.
Each row says what it is doing while you play: not asked for, on and waiting, running, or not used and why.

**Experiment 1: encode each real frame once.** PushFlow's model has a part that looks at each real frame on
its own. Until now that part ran twice for every generated frame, on the same two real frames again and
again. With the experiment on it runs once per real frame and the result is kept, so at 3X and 4X most of
that work is no longer repeated.

- The picture is the same. At start PushFlow makes two test frames both ways on your card and uses the
  shorter way only when they match; if they do not, it says so and carries on as before.
- On the software renderer used for testing, one frame cost 10 to 16 % less. **On real graphics cards the
  saving has not been measured.** The row shows what your card measured at start, for both ways.
- It does not work together with half precision yet, and the row says so when that is why it is not used.
- The download grows by about 45 MB (the model in its split form).

**Experiment 2: Guard.** When an object moves too far between two real frames, PushFlow cannot follow it and
leaves it out of the frame it makes, which shows as flicker on fast objects. Guard looks for those places
and shows the real frame that is nearer in time there. The object then moves at your game's own frame rate
instead of disappearing.

- It never invents a picture: what it shows is always a part of a real frame.
- When more than a third of a frame is affected (a cut between two scenes, for example), the whole frame is
  the real frame. The row counts those apart: frames checked, frames mended in places, frames replaced
  whole.
- A picture PushFlow got right is left exactly as it is. That is tested for a still picture and for an
  object it follows.
- **Its rule was found and checked on drawn test pictures only.** Whether it looks better than the flicker
  in a real game is not known, and it can look blocky where PushFlow had already lost the picture (fine
  textures such as grass or gravel in fast motion are the first place to look). That is for you to judge,
  which is why it is a switch.

**If something goes wrong.** Start `PushFPS.exe --safe-mode` and every experiment is off for that run,
without changing your settings. If PushFPS ever closes unexpectedly while PushFlow starts, the experiments
are switched off at the next start, together with the method, as before.

**Also in this version**

- The performance report (*Settings → Performance report*) says which experiments ran and what they
  measured, so a report from your card tells us what the software renderer cannot.
- The motion size choice reads **Auto**, like every other automatic choice in the app.

### What was tested, and how

- Built and tested automatically on Windows (GitHub's build machines, software renderer): both models with
  and without each experiment, on their own and through the whole frame generation pipeline at 3X.
- **Not tested on a real graphics card or a real game.** The speed of the first experiment and the look of
  the second on real hardware are unknown. If you try them, a performance report helps a lot.
- Nothing in capture, recording, streaming, or the other frame generation methods was changed.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
