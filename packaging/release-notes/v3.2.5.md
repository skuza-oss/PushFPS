## What's new in 3.2.5: Reveal clips, a frame generation watchdog and console streaming fixes

**Reveal: a new comparison clip layout.** The whole picture, full screen, instead of two halves. The clip
opens on the raw console picture at the game's own frame rate. A glowing line then sweeps from left to right
and uncovers PushFPS, holds, sweeps back to the console picture, and repeats for as long as the clip runs.

- Pick **Reveal** under *Layout* in the *Comparison clip* dialog. The dialog's preview plays the sweep.
- **Sweep every** sets how often the line crosses: every 1, 2, 3, 5 or 8 seconds (3 unless you change it).
  A crossing takes between half a second and 1.2 seconds, and eases in and out.
- The line's place comes from each frame's own time in the clip, so it moves evenly whatever the frame rate.
- The badges follow the picture: *PushFPS* at the top left, *Console* at the top right, each cut off at the
  line, so the label always says what you are looking at.
- The lengths work as in every layout: 10 seconds to 2 minutes, or *Until I stop*.

**Clip quality for split and reveal clips.** These two layouts fill the frame, so they can be recorded larger:

- **Auto** (the default): the picture's own size, up to 1440p. Nothing changes for clips you already make.
- **1080p, 1440p, 4K**: recorded at up to that height, with about a third more bitrate than a normal
  recording of that size. A smaller picture is never enlarged.
- A comparison clip records every frame PushFPS shows. At 4K above 60 FPS PushFPS therefore switches the
  NVIDIA encoder to a faster setting so it keeps up. If the graphics card still could not record every frame,
  a message says so when the clip ends, and you can pick one step lower.
- Side by side and vertical clips are exactly as they were.

**PushFPS now says when frame generation is not working.** Frame generation could be switched on while only
the game's own frames reached the screen, with nothing to tell you. PushFPS now counts the frames it really
shows, and when no generated ones come out for ten seconds the quick setup says *No generated frames* and
why: the method is not running, the graphics card does not keep up, or nothing comes out although the method
runs. A still picture such as a menu is not reported, and nothing is changed for you.

**Performance report.** *Settings → Performance report → Copy* (also next to that message) copies what the
session playing now really does: the graphics card and its driver, your settings, the game's own frames and
the generated frames shown each second, and PushFPS's software delay. It holds nothing personal: no user
name, file, device name, address or account. There is a new
[hardware report](https://github.com/DosukaSOL/PushFPS/issues/new?template=hardware-report.yml) to paste it
into, and a guide to [what the numbers mean](https://github.com/DosukaSOL/PushFPS/blob/main/docs/BENCHMARKING.md).

**Tested graphics cards.** The README and website now list the cards PushFPS has been run on with real games:
GeForce RTX 5090, 5080, 5070, 4090, 4080 and 4070, and Radeon RX 9070 XT. It is a compatibility list, not a
speed ranking. Send a hardware report to add yours.

**Fixes for PS5, Xbox and the Elgato 4K X.**

- **PS5 Remote Play: buttons no longer stay pressed.** After fast button combinations a release could get
  lost and the button stayed down in the game. The Remote Play library's own fix for this is now included.
- **Xbox streaming: no more lasting picture corruption.** A damaged frame is rejected and the stream recovers
  at the next clean one, instead of smearing. If corruption still persists, the Xbox dialog has a new
  *Software video decoding* switch (off by default; reconnect after changing it).
- **Elgato 4K X: HDR is detected automatically.** With *Input colour* on Auto the 4K X always stayed in SDR;
  it now reads what the console sends. Other Elgato cards are not affected.

**PushFlow half precision is tied to your graphics card.** A passed half-precision test used to be kept for
good. It now holds for the card, driver and PushFPS version it was measured with; after a new card, a driver
update or a PushFPS update, half precision is switched off, full precision is used, and a message asks you to
test again (*Professional mode → PushFlow half precision*). If you had it on, you will see that message once
after this update.

**A greyed-out AI picture enhancement says how to fix it.** When the graphics card can run it but its files
were left out at installation (they are the installer's optional extras), the quick setup now says so and
how to get them: run `Install-PushFPS.cmd` again and answer **y**. Your settings are kept.

**What comes next** is in the new
[roadmap](https://github.com/DosukaSOL/PushFPS/blob/main/docs/ROADMAP.md). Most of it, the PushFlow speed
work above all, waits for measurements on real graphics cards: every performance report helps.

**Also in this release.** The website and the README were brought up to date with 3.2.x, and both link the
PushFPS Discord: https://discord.gg/N88znNpSgC

**How it was tested.** In CI on Windows without a graphics card: the build; the unit tests (the sweep's timing
for every interval, the line's picture at 720p to 4K, the clip quality rules, the frame generation watchdog
including an hour of healthy play that must never be reported, the performance report and that nothing
personal can get into it, the half-precision rule, the PS5 input fix over a local encrypted connection); the
graphics tests on Microsoft's software renderer, in SDR and HDR (what is left and right of the line, the line
itself, both badges cut at the line, and that split clips are unchanged); the QML tests and the install test.
The PS5 patch was compared with the library's own four commits and is identical to them.
**Not tested on real hardware**: no Reveal clip and no 4K clip was recorded with a capture card, the watchdog's
ten seconds have not been tried against every method on real cards (if a method that works is ever reported,
please send the performance report), and the PS5, Xbox and Elgato 4K X fixes were not tried by us on those
devices: they come with their authors' own tests.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
