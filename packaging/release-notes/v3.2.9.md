## What's new in 3.2.9: Predict, the third PushFlow 2 experiment

**Predict shows the real picture sooner.** Ordinary frame generation makes the frames *between* two real
frames, so it has to wait for the second one: the real picture reaches your screen about one frame late.
Predict does not wait. The newest real frame is shown as soon as it is processed, and the frames after it
are **forecasts**, made from the two real frames that have already arrived.

It is an **experiment that starts off** (*Professional → Frame generation → PushFlow 2: Predict*, with
PushFlow as the method). With it off, nothing changes: every test compares the frames with 3.2.8's.

What to know before you switch it on:

- **It is for live pictures only**: a capture card, a screen, PS5 Remote Play, Moonlight, Xbox. Video files
  and export keep ordinary in-between frames, and the row says so.
- **On paper it saves up to one frame of delay** (33 ms at 30 FPS, 17 ms at 60 FPS). Nobody has measured
  that with a camera yet, so this page claims no figure for your setup.
- **A forecast can be wrong.** Nobody knows the next frame. When something changes direction, appears or
  disappears, the forecast shows what would have happened if it had carried on, until the next real frame
  corrects it. A fast object can overshoot for a moment.
- **Nothing is invented.** Every pixel of a forecast is a pixel of the newest real frame, moved along the
  motion PushFlow found or, where that motion is not trusted, left exactly where it is. A still picture
  and a HUD stay as they are.
- **It costs less.** The network runs once per real frame instead of once per generated frame, so 3X and
  4X are much cheaper than before.
- **Your card has to be quick enough.** At 60 FPS and 2X a forecast must be ready 8 ms after its real
  frame. If your card needs longer, forecasts are skipped and you see the real frames only; the row counts
  what was made and what was shown.
- **Measurements still measure what they did.** While the frame generation quality test or *Optimise for
  my PC* runs, Predict steps aside and comes back afterwards.
- **Recordings and streams are "as played"**: with Predict on they contain the forecasts you saw.

The row shows, while you play: forecasts made, how much of the moving picture was held instead of moved,
and forecasts not shown because the next real frame had already arrived. The performance report
(*Settings → Performance report*) includes the same numbers.

**If something goes wrong**, `PushFPS.exe --safe-mode` starts with every experiment off, for that run.

### What was tested, and how

- Built and tested automatically on Windows (GitHub's build machines, software renderer): the forecast
  against its rule pixel by pixel with both models; through the whole frame generation pipeline at 3X
  (order, timing, and no forecast further from the truth than holding the real frame); and, new in this
  version, **through the whole engine with a live source** fed by the test, once with in-between frames
  and once with Predict.
- **Not tested on a real graphics card, a real capture card or a real game.** How much delay it really
  saves, how even the frames are and how forecasts look in a game are unknown until someone tries. If you
  do, a performance report tells us a lot.
- Nothing in capture, recording, streaming or the other frame generation methods was changed for people
  who leave the switch off.

**Windows Defender note.** Defender may flag new PushFPS downloads as `Trojan:Win32/Tecabans.STV!cl`. This is a false positive from its cloud check on new, unsigned files, and it has been reported to Microsoft. Always check the zip against `SHA256SUMS.txt` on this page.
