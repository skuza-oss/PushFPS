# PushFPS on Steam: store page kit

Everything here is ready to paste or upload into Steamworks. The art is generated by
`scripts/brand/make_steam.py` in the PushFPS brand (graphite, logo green, the classic logo).

## Before you start (owner only)

These steps need the owner's own Steam account and cannot be done from CI:

1. A Steam account that has spent at least US$5, with Steam Guard (mobile authenticator) on.
2. Steamworks onboarding at https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect: digital paperwork, tax
   interview, bank details and identity check. Valve sees your legal name and tax details; they are
   **not** shown on the store. The public developer and publisher name can be `DosukaSOL`.
3. Pay the Steam Direct fee (US$100 per app). It is paid back only after US$1,000 of sales, so for a
   free app it is not paid back.
4. Valve's waiting periods: about 30 days between paying the fee and release, the store page and the
   build are each reviewed (usually 1 to 5 days), and the page must be public as "Coming soon" for at
   least two weeks before release.

## Decisions to make before the first build upload

- **What the Steam build contains.** The GitHub installer downloads Veyra's runtime package from
  Veyra's own release. Steam expects the depot to contain the whole program, so the Steam build would
  ship that runtime itself (Qt, FFmpeg, the NVIDIA/AMD/Intel runtimes Veyra redistributes). Keep the
  same defaults as `packaging/Install-PushFPS.ps1`: only NVIDIA-signed NVIDIA files, no NVIDIA model
  runtimes on AMD; experimental extras stay opt-in.
- **Startup music.** `media/startup-music.mp3` comes from Veyra's package and its notice limits the
  permission to Veyra packages. Leave it out of the Steam depot unless Likely7 gives written
  permission for PushFPS. The intro works without it.
- **ANR.** Never put ANR files in the depot. Users install ANR from Apex Compute through
  *Settings → Components and licenses → Install from download*, as on GitHub.
- **Corresponding source.** Link the GitHub repository and the `streaming-source.zip` of the same
  version in the store description, as on the GitHub releases (GPL-3.0, AGPL-3.0 for the Chiaki part).

## Store page

**Name:** PushFPS

**Type:** Software (Utilities, Video Production). **Price:** Free.

**Short description (≤ 300 characters):**

> Play 30 FPS console games at 60 or 120 FPS. PushFPS reads the picture from your capture card and
> adds generated frames in real time on your PC. Nothing changes on the console or in the game. Free
> and open source, built on Veyra.

**About this software:**

> PushFPS turns your PC into a frame-rate booster for your console. Plug the console into a capture
> card, open PushFPS and pick your game: it shows the picture full screen at 60 or 120 frames per
> second, with generated frames in between the real ones.
>
> **Made for console players**
> - Quick setup: pick your console, the game's frame rate and your target. Three choices instead of hundreds.
> - Optimise for my PC: one click tests every frame generation method, AI enhancement and upscaler on
>   your real picture and keeps the best. Undo puts everything back.
> - HUD that stays sharp: automatic detection keeps the minimap, ammo and timers still.
> - Game presets, a latency readout, before/after comparison clips and recording.
> - No capture card? Stream from PS5 Remote Play, Moonlight/Sunshine or Xbox instead.
> - Works with NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics on Windows 11.
>
> **Credits**
> PushFPS is built on **Veyra** by **Likely7** (https://github.com/Likely7/Veyra-NRVideo), the
> open-source video enhancer that does the heavy lifting. Optional ANR frame generation is by
> **Apex Compute**; you download it from Apex. PushFPS is free software under the GPL-3.0; the source
> code is at https://github.com/DosukaSOL/PushFPS.

**Tags:** Utilities, Video Production, Free to Play, Software, Gaming, Controller.

**System requirements** (same as the README's "What you need"): Windows 11 64-bit with DirectX 12;
any DirectX 12 graphics card for PushFlow (2× to 4×) and FSR frame generation (2×); NVIDIA RTX 30 series
for NVIDIA's methods (RTX 40 or 50 recommended); ANR on GTX 16 and newer, RX 5000 and newer and Intel Arc
(Apex's list); a capture card that shows up as
a camera and sends 1080p60 uncompressed, on a USB 3 port directly on the PC; a 120 Hz screen for 120 FPS.

Avoid on the store page and in capsules: game names or artwork, "DLSS" in headlines, NVIDIA logos,
review scores or awards (Steam rules), and anything that suggests endorsement by a console maker,
NVIDIA or Rockstar Games.
